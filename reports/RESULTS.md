# Experimental results

> Generated by `python -m src.analysis.report_results` from `reports/results.json`. Every number below is measured by `src.evaluation.run_experiments`; none is typed by hand.

## Dataset

- **400** destinations across **107** countries (real data)
- **4000** users and **29852** trips (**synthetic** — see the data provenance note)
- Split: 18704 train / 5574 validation / 5574 test interactions, leave-last-n-out per user

> **What these numbers mean.** The interactions are generated, so the metrics measure how well each model recovers the structure the generator injected (preference archetypes, geographic chaining and popularity bias). That is a genuine learning problem and it validates the pipeline end to end, but it is **not** evidence of real-world recommendation accuracy.

## Popularity bias, coverage and diversity

Accuracy alone cannot distinguish a good recommender from one that shows everybody the same famous cities. Measured at K = 10:

| Learning-to-Rank (LightGBM) | 0.953 | 381 | 0.741 | 0.445 | 0.090 | 1.82 | 0.851 | 6537 |

- **Catalog coverage** — share of destinations that appear in at least one user's top 10.
- **Gini** — concentration of exposure across the catalog (0 = perfectly even, 1 = one destination takes everything).
- **From top decile** — share of recommendations drawn from the most popular 10% of destinations. A high value is the signature of a model that just recommends famous places.
- **Novelty** — mean self-information of what is recommended; higher means less obvious choices.
